## The Wi-Fi name

> **Ex took router, I kept the dog** (30 characters)

A breakup in one line: the ex walked off with the router, the narrator kept the dog. The joke lands on the first read — a divorce settlement where the router and the dog are the assets — and the name implies the whole story: two characters, a split, and who got what.

## How it works

`main.py` (Python 3.13, standard library only) writes the name to `/output/entry.txt`, after self-checking the sealed gate: 1–32 UTF-8 characters, not only whitespace.

## Candidates considered

- `Ex took router, I kept the dog` (30) — chosen: clearest twist, fullest story
- `WiFi died with my marriage` (26) — punchy but thinner story
- `Named after my ex: Unstable` (27) — clever double meaning, little story
- `Mom, stop yelling, I am on Zoom` (31) — vivid scene, no story arc
- `Toddler reset the router again` (30) — funny, no twist
- `Landlord raised rent, cut WiFi` (30) — reads as complaint, weak joke
- `My ex pays for this WiFi now` (28) — funny, less vivid than the pick
